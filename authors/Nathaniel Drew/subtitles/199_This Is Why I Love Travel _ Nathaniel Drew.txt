When I was younger, I used to think that
traveling meant leaving home.
I used to think that it meant seeing new
people and foreign customs.
It meant going to a place where I didn't
understand the language, taking some
pictures, staring at unfamiliar things.
I realize now that all I was seeing was
a piece of what travel means.
That in fact, the word holds much
greater value. If it's really only more
knowledge that I want, more items
checked off the list, I can get that in
my computer from the safety of my room.
Because you know what? For me, travel
doesn't have anything at all to do with
knowledge and everything to do with
understanding. It has to do with
understanding others and it has to do
with understanding myself.
I love travel because it challenges me.
It forces me to think and rethink the
things I thought I already knew. It
makes me question who I am and how I
construct my identity.
It shakes me until I let go of the
things I so dearly grip onto. It puts me
in contact with people that push my
boundaries, that stretch me. Sometimes
it makes me feel silly or dumb because
it makes me look at and acknowledge the
things I don't necessarily like about
myself, the things I'm embarrassed
about. I have no choice but to open my
heart to accept and move forward. Travel
has shown me my strengths and my
weaknesses. It has revealed the things I
love to do. Trouble is fear. Trouble is
love. It means laughing and crying. It's
in the air. It's on the ground. It's you
and it's me.
I used to think that travel meant
leaving home.
I was mistaken.
Travel means finding it.
